Introduction

Longitudinal phenotypes and environmental response

Final biomass or grain yield does not describe when growth occurred or how a genotype responded to changing conditions. Genotypes with similar final performance may differ in their growth histories, and their relative performance can change across environments and during the season.

High-throughput phenotyping provides repeated measurements of growth-related traits. We investigate how these time series can estimate genotype-specific responses and support predictions under different environmental conditions. Our response variable is biomass over time.

An APSIM demonstration dataset

We use daily wheat biomass simulated by Bustos-Korts et al. (2019) with APSIM-Wheat. The study includes 199 genotypes in 124 site-year environments: Emerald, Merredin, Narrabri and Yanco over 1983–2013. Historical environmental information and genotype-specific physiological parameters drive the simulations. These are model-generated phenotypes, rather than field biomass measurements [1].

What APSIM represents

APSIM combines weather, soil, management and genotype-specific physiological parameters to simulate crop and soil processes over time. The schematic summarises its main inputs and outputs [2].

A dynamic model for biomass

We describe growth with an ordinary differential equation. For genotype \(g\) in environment \(e\), let \(B_{ge}(t)\) denote biomass and \(\mathbf u_e(t)\) denote environmental drivers. A model for the rate of change is

$$\frac{dB_{ge}(t)}{dt}=f\!\left(B_{ge}(t),\mathbf u_e(t);\boldsymbol\beta_g\right).$$

The function \(f\) specifies how the current biomass and environmental conditions influence growth. The coefficients \(\boldsymbol\beta_g\) allow responses to differ between genotypes. Given an initial biomass and a driver history, integrating the equation produces a predicted biomass trajectory.

We will estimate a compact approximation using candidate functions of biomass and weather. Later pages will cover estimation and evaluation of trajectory and G×E recovery. The aim is a useful reduced representation, rather than recovery of every APSIM process.

Scope of the analysis

The analysis uses biomass and observed weather, including quantities derived from weather history. It excludes SNP markers and APSIM water-status outputs as predictors; the estimator does not receive the generating physiological parameters. Soil and management effects are present in APSIM but are not fully represented by weather alone, limiting what our reduced model can capture.
