Shared and genotype-specific responses

Genotypes share a population response and have their own deviations. Estimating these responses jointly allows well-observed genotypes to inform genotypes with fewer observations.

\[f_g(\mathbf u)=\boldsymbol\phi(\mathbf u)^\top\boldsymbol\beta+\boldsymbol\phi(\mathbf u)^\top\mathbf b_g,\qquad b_{gk}\mid\lambda_{Gk}\sim N(0,\lambda_{Gk}^{-1}).\]

The zero mean is a prior on genotype deviations; it is not a constraint forcing the fitted deviations to sum to zero. We interpret complete genotype responses as well as their shared component.

What does pooling change?

In a real-data exercise, g008, g052 and g185 have only one or three training environments. Nine other genotypes retain their training data. Both methods are evaluated on the same held-out years for the three sparsely observed genotypes.

Location extension
\[f_{g\ell}(\mathbf u)=\boldsymbol\phi(\mathbf u)^\top(\boldsymbol\beta+\mathbf b_g+\mathbf c_\ell).\]

Location deviations use sum-to-zero contrasts and a fixed Gaussian shrinkage prior. They describe persistent differences among known locations. This compact extension omits the separate genotype–location offset in the research slides.
